How it was measured

  1. Two pictures from space

    The Landsat 8 satellite photographed the valley on , before the flood, and again on , during it. Each image is a grid of squares m across.

  2. Find the water

    Water absorbs shortwave infrared light that land reflects. A simple ratio of green to shortwave infrared light (the MNDWI index) marks a square as water when it is above , a cut-off found automatically from the flood-day picture.

  3. Set aside what cannot be seen

    Squares hidden by cloud, cloud shadow or snow on either date are left out and reported, not guessed.

  4. Compare before and during

    A square that is water in September but was dry in August counts as flooded. Water on both dates, the river and the lakes, does not. Only the valley floor below m that connects to Srinagar is measured.

  5. Count what stands on it

    Today's road map and building outlines are laid over the flood map, and the roads and buildings on flooded squares are counted.

The rules for each step were written down and committed before the result was computed. Every choice and number is in METHODS.md.

What this cannot tell you

  • One moment, not the whole flood. The image was taken at UTC on . Water rose and fell over many days; this is not the largest extent.
  • The old city is probably undercounted. Where roofs fill most of a 30 m square, water in the lanes between them may not register. Dense blocks often appear dry inside flooded ground. This was not tested.
  • Lakes covered by plants. In August parts of Wular and Dal were covered by plants and looked like land. Where they were open water in September they count as flooded, which is why some flooding lies inside lakes and wetlands.
  • Today's maps, not 2014's. Roads come from OpenStreetMap as of . Buildings come from images of –. Some of these buildings did not exist in 2014; some that did are gone. The building figure is not a count of buildings flooded.
  • Clouds the satellite's own mask missed hide what is under them, roughly km² by a rough estimate.
  • The cut-off matters. Moving the water cut-off within a reasonable range changes the flooded area from to km².
  • Only the valley floor was measured, below m and connected to Srinagar. Flooding elsewhere is not counted. The elevation model includes buildings and trees.
